## Day 2 — Tue Oct 27
- Muse Spark Open (D4): the fix-plan agent now runs on Meta's open-weight model on Baseten (`meta-models/muse-spark-open-1026`) with the same tools, verifier and trace, and falls back to hosted Muse Spark if it fails, gives no valid plan, or would pass the scan's spend limit. The report shows which model planned. Benchmark on the real Constellation scan: 3 of 3 valid plans on each model, median 64 s (open) vs 68 s (hosted), about $0.08 vs $0.002 per plan (`docs/benchmarks.md`). "Built with Muse Spark Open" in the report and README.
- Muse Image after pictures (E8): the top 3 fixes get the clean video frame of their closest measured hazard edited to show only that fix ("remove the dry leaves along the wall; keep everything else identical"), shown as Now / After the fix next to the fix and labelled as an AI illustration, never evidence. $0.01 each; a failed picture never fails the scan.
- Privacy guard (official rule: no people, blur anything incidental before storing): SAM 3.1 also looks for "person" and "license plate" in the same upload; a new privacy stage pixelates and blurs every box where one was seen (with a margin, filling short gaps in a track) in all frames before evidence images or Muse Image pictures are made, and the raw and intermediate videos, audio and corner frames are deleted when the scan ends. It fails closed: if the check can't run, the scan keeps nothing. It is code, not a model choice, and shows as the first step of the agent trace, with a loop strip (privacy guard, plan, tool calls, verify, picture) marking the guardrails. Baseten spend now has its own per-run cap and no longer counts toward the Meta account caps.
- After pictures are checked: Muse Spark compares each before/after pair and drops a picture that shows no change or misrepresents its fix (an inspection fix pictured as removal), and the picture uses a hazard that is clearly in view. Now / After outline the hazards the scan found.
- Building cover photos: an aerial photo around each researched address (USGS NAIP, public domain, basemap fallback), on the Buildings and Reports cards and the report header. No pin, since geocoded points often land on the street.
- Coverage, not false passes: the rules engine knows how far out the walkaround covered (about 10 ft from the walls, or the farthest measured hazard). A rule whose area lies wholly beyond that (most of Zone 2) is "Not covered by this video, check on site" and doesn't move the score, instead of passing because nothing was seen; a pass that reaches beyond it says how far the video covered. The zone map hatches the ground the video didn't cover.
- Evidence "hover for why" and thumbnail zoom react only to their own image, not the whole section.
- Corner clips removed. Eave depth was their only use, it only feeds two proposed Zone 0 rules, and Muse Spark's estimate from video was a rough range. Each building now has No overhang / I know it (ft) / Not sure; "Not sure" makes those two rules needs-review. The eaves pipeline stage is gone and filming is one walkaround. Buildings set to "Measure from video" become "Not sure" (migration 0002).
- California only, said plainly: the README, the New scan page and the community form say so, and address research records the state. An address outside California is flagged on the building, the New scan form and the report, and research doesn't apply California law to it.
- Hazard-zone lookup is statewide: address research reads CAL FIRE / State Fire Marshal's own zone maps (SRA, and the 2025 LRA map) instead of LA County's, so any California address gets its zone. Checked at Valencia (Very High LRA, same as LA County's map), Julian (Very High SRA), Paradise (Very High LRA) and downtown Sacramento (no zone).

## Day 1 (continued)
- Spend tracking (F3, part 1): Meta price table, cost functions for tokens, SAM frames (50-frame minimum), audio seconds and images, and a per-run ledger that blocks a call before it would exceed `SPEND_LIMIT_USD_PER_RUN`. 10 tests.
- `docs/how-measurement-works.md`: how the printed marker, homography and uncertainty ranges turn video into feet.
- Video pipeline (B1): ffprobe/ffmpeg wrappers to probe, normalize to H.264 720p, extract frames with timestamps, and extract 16 kHz mono WAV for Voice Transcribe. Tests use synthetic clips only; CI installs ffmpeg.
- Keyframe selection (B2): drop blurry frames (variance of Laplacian) and near-duplicates (dHash), cap the count while keeping coverage. Checked on a real clip: 16 frames to 8 keyframes in about a second.
- SAM 3.1 tracking (C1): Meta API client (file upload, streamed Responses), segment_video with spend checks and billed-frame recording, and a Node helper running Meta's official SAM parser (about 70x faster than the Python build: 0.7 s vs 46 s for 24 frames). First live run: bark mulch tracked as one object across all 24 frames, 0.78 confidence, 50 frames billed ($0.01). Replay tests from the recorded response; `normalize` can lower fps to cut SAM cost.
- Rules engine (D1): deterministic evaluation of the us-ca pack against measured hazards (distance ranges, zone uncertainty, unobserved sides), with pass / fail / needs-review per rule and a score. 45 tests.
- Scan pipeline and worker service: normalize, frames, SAM 3.1 (12 hazard concepts in parallel, one upload), measure, rules, narration (Voice Transcribe), agent, report. FastAPI service with live stage progress. First live end-to-end scan: 8 objects tracked, 39 rules checked, 5 fixes, $0.12.
- Muse Spark agent (D2/D3): reads rule results, hazards and narration through tool calls, submits a fix plan, and a code-side verifier rejects plans that cite unknown rules or leave a failed rule uncovered (one retry, then a deterministic fallback plan). Every step is kept for the report's trace.
- Measurement (C2/C3/C5): scale-marker detection and ground homography with Monte Carlo uncertainty, focal-length estimate across frames, wall distance per hazard track, zone assignment that flags boundary cases, and annotated evidence images (mask, wall line, distance). Printable 6x8 marker PDF. 3.0 ft synthetic hazard measured as 2.8-3.2 ft.
- Address research agent: add a street address to a building and Muse Spark looks it up with tool calls (US Census geocoder, LA County 2025 Fire Hazard Severity Zones, CAL FIRE fire perimeters, our rule reference) and writes a cited note: jurisdiction, hazard zone, which law applies, recent fires, what to confirm. Code drops any fact citing a source the agent never looked up. First run: 28422 Constellation Rd, Very High LRA, Gov. Code 51182, Hughes Fire 2025 within 5 miles, $0.0008. Account-wide spend caps (24 h, total, concurrency) and an optional scan passcode guard the Meta account.
- Multi-clip scans: several walkaround clips are joined (mixed orientation, silent clips padded) into one SAM 3.1 run, and optional corner clips go to a new eaves stage where Muse Spark reads the roof overhang against the marker from frames spread across each clip and returns a range (an entered depth always wins). First run: 2 clips joined, eaves about 1-2.5 ft (low confidence), $0.13.
- First real-footage scan (28422 Constellation Rd, 3 takes, 48 s): 16 objects tracked, 7 of 15 hazards measured with the marker, first real fail (dry leaves 1.0-1.3 ft from the wall), score 78, $0.35. Fixes it needed: portrait video was being shrunk to 405x720 (now short side 720), marker found at full resolution and mapped onto SAM frames, marker fit limit 2 px (real sheets on uneven ground), ranges wider than 6 ft treated as unmeasured, budget-aware SAM frame rate, no-overhang eaves.
